Water tracker — Home surface

Design gate for WATER-1. Four concepts (two orientations × two sizes), four states each, both Erewhon themes. Nothing ships until §1 tokens and §3 concept are ruled. Layout + motion only — no implementation guidance.

How to read this page. Neutral hexes are transcribed from ThemeColors.erewhonDark / .erewhonLight and are not up for debate — where a hex here disagrees with the token, the token wins. The --water-* family in §1 is the only new colour proposed, and it is the thing being ruled on. Every tile below reads its colours from those variables; there are no loose hexes in the element markup.

Placement. Flat (Erewhon) Home arm, between calorieHero and jumpCards — directly below the daily rings/summary. Neighbours are drawn as dashed gray blocks in §2. The pixel arm is out of scope for this gate.

§1Water tokens — proposed, for ruling

⚠ The conflict this palette exists to solve

Erewhon's accent is already blue. #4882FF (dark) / #083ACE (light), hue ≈ 221°. It is carrying the calorie ring, the XP fill, the protein macro bar, the tab-active state and every primary button. A water element placed directly beneath the calorie hero in that same blue would read as one more accent bar — the one thing this element cannot afford, since its whole job is to be glanceable at a distance on a screen actively being de-cluttered.

So the proposal is cyan-leaning, ~192° — a deliberate ~30° hue separation from the accent. Far enough to read as a different substance at a glance; close enough to stay inside the same cool family and not fight the palette. It also clears the existing greens (#429C5A, 137°) and ambers (#E0B047, 43°), so no purity band is ambiguous against it.

Alternative if the ruling is "no new colour": the element can run entirely on --accent + --sunk. It costs the glance-distinction above and makes the water read as a second progress bar; the wave/meniscus still work. Say the word and I'll add that arm.

Concept C — vertical, primary · adds ~112 pt

The compactness argument, stated plainly. Concept A costs ~72 pt and keeps the horizontal rhythm every neighbouring block already uses (purity, macros and the XP track are all left-to-right capsule fills). Concept C costs ~112 pt — ~40 pt more — and interrupts that rhythm with the only vertical form on the screen. That interruption is the entire argument for it: it is the most water-like and the most memorable, and the wave is legible in a way a leading-edge meniscus never quite is. On a screen being de-cluttered, A is the safer read and C is the more delightful one. B and D below are the minimal reductions of each.

§4Motion — decorative vs required

The CSS above is an approximation of the intended feel, not a specification of technique. What is being ruled on is the column marked Required, the durations, and the Reduce-Motion degradation.

MotionRequired?Timing Amplitude / curveNotes
Level change
fill grows to the new cup count		 REQUIRED 220 ms Spring, slight overshoot then settle This is the state feedback — the one motion that must survive every setting. Never blocks input; a second tap mid-animation retargets rather than queueing.
Splash burst
droplets + ripple at the surface		 REQUIRED starts 0 ms · ends ≤ 400 ms
(budget: 600 ms)		 5 droplets, 9–23 pt travel, staggered 0–66 ms; ripple scales 0.35→1.9 over 300 ms from +40 ms Non-blocking and non-interactive — purely additive over the level change. Comes in comfortably under the 600 ms target, which leaves headroom if the ruling wants it slower.
Haptic REQUIRED fires at 0 ms, with the tap Light impact On the tap that reaches the goal (→ 8/8), a success haptic instead. No haptic beyond the goal — cups 9, 10, 11… get the light impact again, so overfull never feels like a repeated achievement.
Surface wave (vertical)
crest undulating at the waterline		 decorative 6.2 s + 9.4 s, infinite, counter-rotating ±≈3 pt vertical, linear Two layers at different periods so it never visibly loops. Hidden entirely at 0 cups (no water, no surface).
Meniscus bob (horizontal)
the leading-edge surface		 decorative 2.6 s, infinite ±1.6 pt horizontal, ease-in-out The horizontal analogue of the wave. Hidden at 0 cups.
Specular sheen decorative 3.4 s, infinite Soft highlight sweeping the fill body, ease-in-out The "is it water or is it a progress bar" tiebreaker. Cheapest thing on the page to cut if it reads busy.
Goal-met glow decorative 500 ms, one-shot Ring expands 0→9 pt and fades Fires once, only on the transition into 8/8. Not on load, not on 9/8.
Reduce Motion

Toggle it in the toolbar to see the degraded behaviour on every tile. All decorative motion stops: the wave freezes mid-crest (a still waterline, not a flat one), the sheen and bob stop, the goal glow does not fire. The level change survives — it is state feedback, not decoration. The splash degrades to a single 180 ms brightness pulse at the surface: no droplets, no ripple, but the tap is still unmistakably acknowledged, and the haptic is unaffected.

§5State rules

StateVesselReading + affordances
Empty
0 / 8		 Bare track/vessel at --sunk. No fill, no meniscus, no wave — there is no surface without water, and an animating empty vessel is a lie. Count reads 0 / 8 in --ink (not dimmed — it is a real value, not a placeholder). All 8 pips unfilled. No empty-state copy; the label and the +1 are the whole affordance.
Partial
5 / 8		 Fill to 62.5%, full motion set. The default. Pips 1–5 filled.
Goal met
8 / 8		 Fill at 100%. Wave still runs (it does not stop at goal). One-shot glow on arrival only. All 8 pips filled. +1 stays enabled — the goal is a target, not a cap.
Overfull
9 / 8		 Fill is clamped at 100% forever. Above goal the surface gains a brighter water-mist rim — "brimming", not "broken". Count keeps counting truthfully: 9 / 8, then 10, 11… A keyline +1 chip (2 pt left rule, no fill — the GUILD-UI-1 keyline-tag language) sits after the count. Pips: all 8 filled, the last carrying an over-ring. Never danger, never a warning — 9 cups of water is not an error, and this element must never scold.
Daily reset

The count resets to 0 at local midnight, matching how the rest of the day rolls over. The primary variants carry a permanent micro-label — RESETS 12:00 AM in --faint — so the rule is discoverable without a tap. The minimal variants drop it; that is part of what makes them minimal, and it is a real cost worth weighing in the ruling.

Two things this mockup deliberately does not decide, because they are behaviour and belong in the WATER-1 prompt, not a layout gate: whether the goal (8) is user-configurable, and whether there is any way to remove a mis-tapped cup. Flagging the second one loudly — a one-tap incrementer with no undo is a real usability hole, and the two obvious answers (long-press to decrement, or a −1 that appears once count > 0) have different layout costs. Neither is drawn here. It needs a ruling before the layout is locked.

§6Open for ruling

  • 1 · Tokens. Adopt water / water-deep / water-mist at ~192° as drawn? Or run the element on --accent and accept that it reads as another blue bar?
  • 2 · Concept. A, B, C or D. (A = safest and rhymes with its neighbours; C = most distinctive, costs ~40 pt more; B and D are the reductions.)
  • 3 · Trough height — only if A or B wins. A uses 14 pt, not the 8 pt that purityBlock and the macro bars use. The extra 6 pt is what makes the surface legible as water at all; at 8 pt (as B draws it) the meniscus is a rim highlight and the "water" reading gets thin. Is breaking the 8 pt track convention acceptable here?
  • 4 · Cup pips. Keep (discrete, countable without reading, and the colour-blind fallback) or drop for density? Present in A and C, absent in B and D.
  • 5 · Undo. No way to remove a mis-tapped cup is currently drawn. Long-press to decrement, a conditional −1, or explicitly none?
  • 6 · Splash budget. Drawn at ≤400 ms against the ≤600 ms target. Keep it snappy, or spend the remaining 200 ms on a slower, more liquid arc?

Rulings on §6 go into the WATER-1 implementation prompt, which is drafted only after this file is locked.
