Water tracker — Home surface

Design gate for WATER-1. Four concepts (two orientations × two sizes), four states each, both Erewhon themes. Nothing ships until §1 tokens and §3 concept are ruled. Layout + motion only.

How to read this page. Neutral hexes are transcribed from ThemeColors.erewhonDark / .erewhonLight and are not up for debate — where a hex here disagrees with the token, the token wins. The --water-* family in §1 is the only new colour proposed. Every tile reads its colours from those variables; there are no loose hexes in the element markup.

The tiles are live. Each state label is a starting state — tapping the droplet raises that tile's count for real, so the fill, the wavy surface, the pips and the overfull chip all move together. ↺ Reset in the toolbar puts every tile back to its labelled state.

Placement. Flat (Erewhon) Home arm, between calorieHero and jumpCards — directly below the daily rings/summary. Neighbours are drawn as dashed gray blocks in §2. The pixel arm is out of scope for this gate.

§1Water tokens — proposed, for ruling

⚠ The conflict this palette exists to solve

Erewhon's accent is already blue. #4882FF (dark) / #083ACE (light), hue ≈ 221°. It is carrying the calorie ring, the XP fill, the protein macro bar, the tab-active state and every primary button. A water element placed directly beneath the calorie hero in that same blue would read as one more accent bar — the one thing this element cannot afford, since its whole job is to be glanceable at a distance on a screen actively being de-cluttered.

So the proposal is cyan-leaning, ~192° — a deliberate ~30° hue separation from the accent. Far enough to read as a different substance at a glance; close enough to stay inside the same cool family and not fight the palette. It also clears the existing greens (#429C5A, 137°) and ambers (#E0B047, 43°), so no purity band is ambiguous against it.

Rev 5 restored the margin. Very light cyan sits at ~190°, so the gap to the 221° accent is back to ~31° — wider than any revision so far, and the water is also far lighter than the accent (72% vs 60% lightness in dark). With the caustic network back, the water is again the only element on Home that is textured, wavy-edged and pip-counted. Nothing else in Erewhon looks remotely like it, and none of that reading depends on hue, so the colour-blind case is covered structurally rather than by the palette. The rev-4 worry about the wave being load-bearing is resolved: it now has the texture sharing the load again.

Alternative if the ruling is "no new colour": the element can run entirely on --accent + --sunk. It costs the glance-distinction above and makes the water read as a second progress bar; the caustic, wave and splash all still work. Say the word and I'll add that arm.

⚠ Why the light column is not the same sky blue

"Very light cyan" is the design intent and it is drawn exactly that way in Erewhon Dark, where it sits on a near-black page and reads at 12.3:1. Erewhon Light is the problem: the page is #F3F4F6 and the empty track is #E8EAED, both essentially white. The dark column's #7BDFF2 on that track measures 1.3:1 — a filled bar would be almost indistinguishable from an empty one in sunlight or for anyone with reduced contrast sensitivity, and the fill level is the entire point of the element.

So the light column keeps the cyan hue (192°, same family) and takes it down to #17849F, landing at 3.6:1 against the track. It still reads as cyan against white; it is simply not pale. This mirrors what the palette already does elsewhere: m-carbs is #F37513 in dark and #CD4B00 in light, m-fat #429C5A / #0F6A31. The pale cyan in light mode is available if you want it — but it costs the fill/empty distinction, and that should be said out loud rather than discovered later. It is ruling item 8.

§2In context — Home, both orientations

Dashed gray blocks are the real neighbours, sketched. Only the water element is drawn for real. Both frames are Erewhon Dark, starting at 5/8 — tap the droplets.

The compactness argument, stated plainly. Concept A costs ~76 pt and keeps the horizontal rhythm every neighbouring block already uses (purity, macros and the XP track are all left-to-right capsule fills). Concept C costs ~132 pt — ~56 pt more — and interrupts that rhythm with the only vertical form on the screen. That interruption is the entire argument for it: it is the most water-like and the most memorable, and with the caustic texture the vertical vessel is where the reference image actually reads. On a screen being de-cluttered, A is the safer choice and C is the more delightful one.

§4Motion — decorative vs required

The CSS here is an approximation of the intended feel, not a specification of technique. What is being ruled on is the Required column, the durations, and the Reduce-Motion degradation.

✓ How the texture got cheap — and what that means for the build

This went three ways before it landed, and the path matters more than the destination because it is the same decision the implementation faces.

Rev 2/3 ran the filter live. The caustic is band-passed fractal noise — a noise field thresholded to a narrow luminance band, which is what turns clouds into a ribbon web. No arrangement of gradients produces it. So each water layer carried its own feTurbulence, 68 in all, each with its frequency animated. feTurbulence is a per-pixel CPU filter: animating it forces a full re-rasterize every frame, 68 times over. That is what made the button splash feel blocky — the animation was correct, the main thread just never had a frame to spare.

Rev 4 removed the texture to fix the stutter, and lost the look.

Rev 5 keeps both by separating generating the texture from animating it. The identical filter is baked once into a small stitchTiles SVG, which the browser rasterizes a single time and then tiles as an ordinary background image. Nothing animates but transform. One raster, for the whole page, forever — instead of 68 per frame. The loop also got better: a tiled image can translate by exactly one tile and wrap invisibly, where the live version had to ping-pong because a noise field is not tileable.

The conclusion to carry into WATER-1: the answer to the old shader-vs-asset question is asset — but a generated one, not a hand-authored one. The texture is produced from the token values rather than drawn by hand, so it restyles with the theme and there is no artwork to keep in sync. Ruling item 7 stands closed; what replaces it is a note that the texture must be rendered once and reused, never evaluated per frame. Getting that backwards is precisely what made revs 2 and 3 stutter.

Reduce Motion

Toggle it in the toolbar to see the degraded behaviour on every tile. All decorative motion stops: the caustic freezes (the texture stays — only its drift and boil stop), the wave holds mid-crest as a still wavy line rather than snapping flat, the meniscus stops, the goal glow does not fire. The level change survives — it is state feedback, not decoration. The splash degrades to a single 180 ms brightness pulse on the button: no crown, no ripples, no dip — nothing that moves or scales — but the tap is still unmistakably acknowledged, and the haptic is unaffected.

Rev 3 needed an extra step here: the caustic boil was an SVG filter animation rather than a CSS one, so switching off CSS animation did not reach it and the texture kept churning while everything else had stopped. With the texture now pure CSS there is no such gap left — but it is worth carrying the lesson into the implementation prompt, because "we turned off the animations" is exactly the kind of claim that passes a casual check while something keeps running underneath it.

§5State rules

StateVesselReading + affordances
Empty
0 / 8		 Bare track/vessel at --sunk. No fill, no texture, no wave — there is no surface without water, and an animating empty vessel is a lie. This falls out for free: the wave carve is drawn in the track colour, so with no water there is nothing to carve. Count reads 0 / 8 in --ink (not dimmed — it is a real value, not a placeholder). All 8 pips unfilled. No empty-state copy; the label and the droplet are the whole affordance.
Partial
5 / 8		 Fill to 62.5%, full texture and wave. The default. Pips 1–5 filled.
Goal met
8 / 8		 Fill at 100% and the water goes still. The wave fades out over 320 ms and the box fills flat to the brim — no surface, no motion. One-shot glow on arrival only. All 8 pips filled. The droplet stays enabled — the goal is a target, not a cap. The stillness is the only "you did it" signal; there is deliberately no badge, toast or copy change.
Overfull
9 / 8		 Fill is clamped at 100% forever, and stays still like the full state. The vessel then lights up: the body brightens to water-neon at the top, a neon hairline runs inside the rim, and a soft neon glow sits outside it, breathing slowly. Read as "brimming", never as "broken" — it is the most celebratory the element ever gets, which is deliberate, because drinking more water than the goal is a good outcome and the surface should look pleased about it. Count keeps counting truthfully: 9 / 8, then 10, 11… A keyline +N chip (2 pt left rule, no fill — the GUILD-UI-1 keyline-tag language) sits after the count. Pips: all 8 filled, the last carrying an over-ring. Never danger, never a warning — 9 cups of water is not an error, and this element must never scold. Tap past 9 on any tile to watch the chip keep counting while the water does not.
Daily reset

The count resets to 0 at local midnight, matching how the rest of the day rolls over. The primary variants carry a permanent micro-label — RESETS 12:00 AM in --faint — so the rule is discoverable without a tap. The minimal variants drop it; that is part of what makes them minimal, and it is a real cost worth weighing in the ruling.

Two things this mockup deliberately does not decide, because they are behaviour and belong in the WATER-1 prompt, not a layout gate: whether the goal (8) is user-configurable, and whether there is any way to remove a mis-tapped cup. Flagging the second one loudly — now that the button is live you can feel it: there is no way back down. A one-tap incrementer with no undo is a real usability hole, and the two obvious answers (long-press to decrement, or a −1 that appears once count > 0) have different layout costs. Neither is drawn here. It needs a ruling before the layout is locked.

§6Pixel arm — the water, pixelated

Home has two renderers. Everything above is the flat (Erewhon) arm; the pixel arm is a different drawing, not a filter over the same one. Rendered below at 4× so the grid is legible — every edge sits on a whole pixel and nothing is anti-aliased. These tiles are live too: tap a droplet to fill one.

The geometry is chosen, not guessed. The column is 9 × 42 units, which leaves an inner well of 7 × 40 — and 40 divides by 8, so one cup is exactly 5 pixel-units. The fill never lands on a half pixel and never needs rounding, which is the whole reason to work on a grid. At 4× that is a 36 × 168 pt column, 1:4.7, sitting between the Erewhon vessel (1:4.1) and the pixel arm's existing PurityVerticalBar (24×150, 1:6.3).

The button is the same control in the other medium. A hand-set 13×13 pixel disc — rasterising a circle at this size looks wrong unless the shoulders are placed by eye — with the droplet knocked out in the outline colour and a darker underside so it reads as an object rather than a sticker. The press is a 1-unit sink, not a scale: scaling a sprite resamples it and the whole thing goes soft, which is the one thing pixel art cannot survive.

The motion rule inverts here, and it matters. Everything in §4 is about making animation smooth — interpolated, sub-pixel, compositor-driven. In the pixel arm that is wrong. The wave and the caustic move in whole-pixel steps (steps(8)), the overfull keyline blinks between two states rather than breathing through a gradient, and there is no glow at all because a soft glow cannot be drawn on a pixel grid. Chunky frames are a bug in the Erewhon arm and the medium in this one — worth stating plainly, because "make it smoother" is the exact wrong note to give this variant.

It also rhymes with something that already exists. The pixel Home already has a tall thin vertical bar: PurityVerticalBar, 24×150 with a 2 pt border, a flat track and a banded fill. The pixel water is built to that family — same proportion, same border weight, same hard track — so it reads as a sibling rather than a transplant. That is also why the Erewhon vessel got narrower: the two arms should be the same object.

Two things the grid forced, worth knowing before this is built. Each crown droplet travels a whole number of units and gets steps() equal to that number, so every frame lands exactly on the grid; a 20 pt travel over 4 steps would put frames on 5 pt boundaries, which is off-grid at a 4 pt unit and resamples every one of them. And the crown is drawn in deep water, not foam — the droplets fly clear of the disc and land on the card, and foam-white is invisible on parchment. The night card flips it back to foam. Colour that is chosen against the wrong surface is the commonest way a pixel sprite reads as broken.

§7Putting it on Home — the Tools toggle

The water tracker is opt-in: it does not appear on Home until you switch it on from Tools. That is a good call for a screen being de-cluttered — it means shipping this costs nobody any Home space unless they ask for it. Two readings of "secret toggle" are drawn below, because they lead to different designs.

⚠ One thing to settle before this can be specced

Tools is not on Home — it is on the Log tab (FoodLogView, opened as a sheet from the date-nav row). So "put the water tracker on the Home screen from the Tools menu" is a control on one tab that changes another. That works, and it is where a power-user toggle belongs, but it is worth naming: the toggle is not discoverable from the screen it affects. If the intent is that people find this on their own, the Home surface would need an entry point of its own — which is a different, bigger change.

Also flagging that "secret" is doing real work in the request and I can read it two ways. A takes it as "a small extra toggle tucked in Tools" — visible, just not a headline. B takes it literally, matching the SECRET-1 hidden-settings pattern already in the app (a ??? section that only appears once you have found it). They are drawn side by side.

§8The Tools entry point

⚠ Scope note — this one is not really a water change

Making Tools noticeable changes the Log tab for everyone, whether or not they ever touch water. It is drawn here because it came up with the water work and it is cheap to show, but it wants its own prompt and its own PR — bundling a nav change into a feature diff is how both become unreviewable. Ruling on it here is fine; shipping it in WATER-1 is not what I would do.

Why it goes unnoticed today is not a mystery once you look at it: it is a bare wrench.and.screwdriver glyph at 17 pt in textSecondary, with no label, no border, no fill and no background, sitting as a trailing overlay on the date-nav row. It has a 44 pt tap target, so it is reachable — it just does not read as a control. Three ways to fix that, in ascending loudness:

  • 1 · Tokens. Adopt water / water-deep / water-foam at ~192° as drawn? Or run the element on --accent and accept that it reads as another blue bar?
  • 2 · Concept. A, B, C or D. (A = safest and rhymes with its neighbours; C = most distinctive and the best home for the texture, costs ~36 pt more; B and D are the reductions.)
  • 3 · Trough height — only if A or B wins. A now uses 18 pt against the 8 pt that purityBlock and the macro bars use.   • 4 · Cup pips. Keep (discrete, countable without reading, and the colour-blind fallback) or drop for density? Present in A and C, absent in B and D.
  • 5 · Undo. Still not drawn. Long-press to decrement, a conditional −1, or explicitly none?
  • 6 · Splash budget. Drawn at ≤410 ms against the ≤600 ms target. Keep it snappy, or spend the remaining ~190 ms on a slower, more liquid arc?
  • 7 · The texture. closed The caustic network is back and now costs a single rasterization for the whole page instead of a live filter per element. Nothing left to rule on — the constraint that replaced it is an implementation note, not a choice: render the texture once and reuse it, never per frame. Kept here so the history stays visible rather than silently dropped.
  • 8 · Light-theme water depth. The light column is a deeper sky blue (#2189C4) because the dark column’s pale blue measures 1.5:1 against the near-white empty track — the fill/empty distinction would be lost.
